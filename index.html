RESEARCH OVERVIEW

Abstract

Working draft

Real-world garment manipulation remains challenging due to severe deformation, self-occlusion, and uncertain physical interactions. While foundation models enable flexible manipulation without task-specific policy training, repeated action planning incurs substantial reasoning overhead, and the experience gained from physical interactions is rarely consolidated into transferable manipulation skills.

We present Intention–Outcome Discrepancy-Driven Skill Discovery, an experience-driven agent harness for reducing reasoning latency and enabling cross-garment generalization. Our key insight is that discrepancies between an agent’s original manipulation intention and its observed physical outcome reveal task-relevant effects overlooked during planning. Through hypothesis-guided exploration and skill induction, the discovered action–effect relationships are consolidated into reusable, observation-conditioned manipulation skills.

During deployment, the harness grounds acquired skills in the current garment observation, replacing open-ended action planning with skill-guided action generation. We evaluate our approach on real-world garment unfolding, focusing on reasoning latency, skill acquisition, and generalization to unseen garments, while keeping the foundation model frozen.
